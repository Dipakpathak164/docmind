Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a powerful paradigm in artificial intelligence that enhances the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by integrating external knowledge sources. While modern LLMs are trained on vast amounts of data, they are static and can hallucinate or lack access to proprietary, private, or real-time information. RAG addresses this limitation by partitioning the process into two key phases: retrieval and generation.

In the retrieval phase, the system searches an external corpus—such as documents, databases, or websites—to locate relevant information matching the user's query. This search typically employs dense vector embeddings, representing queries and document chunks as high-dimensional coordinates in vector space to find semantic matches. Once the most relevant text chunks are retrieved, they are consolidated.

In the generation phase, the retrieved information is combined with the user's original query and presented to the LLM as context. The model then synthesizes this context to produce a precise, coherent, and factually grounded response. By grounding responses directly in external documents, RAG minimizes hallucinations, guarantees high-fidelity answers, and enables domain-specific queries without the astronomical computational costs associated with fine-tuning the LLM.
